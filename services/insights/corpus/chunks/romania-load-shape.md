National system load (Electricity Maps, 2025 data):

- Annual load 51.8 TWh in 2025, down 4.1% from 54.0 TWh in 2024. Romania was a net importer
  (4.2 TWh), imports peaking at 17:00.
- **Daily shape**: average minimum 5.1 GW at 01:00, average peak 7.1 GW at 17:00. Weekdays
  average 6.1 GW, weekends 5.4 GW (about 11% lower).
- **Extremes**: highest hourly load 8.9 GW (6 Oct and 24 Nov 2025, 16:00); lowest 2.4 GW
  (20 Apr 2025, 11:00, Easter Sunday with strong solar).
- **Evening ramp** (net of wind and solar) is steepest in summer: 2.9 GW from the 09:00 belly
  to the 18:00 peak; winter ramp 2.1 GW.
- **Winter records**: consumption above 9.5 GW on a cold January evening in 2021 (9,562 MW at
  17:50, with 1.5 GW imported). The historical maximum cited was 9,930 MW (January 2017).

**What a "peak day" means for a distribution operator**
- Peak days are cold winter evenings (heating, lighting coincide at 17:00–19:00) or heatwave
  afternoons (air conditioning). A storm day is different: load may fall while outages spike.
- On a peak day, interval meters show a higher and earlier evening peak; on a storm day, many
  meters in one area go silent at once.

**How the analyst uses this**
- Compare a peak day with the same weekday of a normal week; a weekday against a Sunday
  overstates the difference by about 11% on its own.
- Check the shape of simulated meter data against this one: night trough, 17:00 peak. If the
  simulator's profile has no evening peak, say the grid conclusions are limited.
